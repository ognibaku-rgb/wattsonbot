01 / The habit

You park, turn on Sentry, and... hope.

Sentry Mode is one of Tesla's best security features, but confirming it's actually active means opening the app and checking the toggle — every single time you park somewhere you care about. It's a small habit that's easy to skip when you're in a hurry, and easy to forget to double-check.

  • 🅿️Parked in an unfamiliar area. You want confirmation Sentry actually engaged — not just that you tapped the toggle.
  • 🔄Sentry got disabled unexpectedly. Low battery, a setting change, or a software update can turn it off without you noticing.
  • 📱Checking means opening the app. One more app, one more login prompt, one more thing to remember mid-errand.

WattsonBot removes the "hope" part — you get a message the moment the state actually changes, in the same chat you're already in.

What it doesn't do

WattsonBot alerts on the Sentry Mode on/off state — it doesn't access or forward the camera recordings themselves. Those stay on the car's USB drive and in the Tesla app, exactly as Tesla designed it. Read-only access also means it can't toggle Sentry Mode for you; it only observes and reports.

04 / FAQ

Fair questions.

Does it alert when Sentry Mode turns on?

Yes — a Telegram message is sent whenever Sentry Mode is enabled or disabled, so you can confirm it activated without opening the app.

Can it turn Sentry Mode on or off for me?

No. WattsonBot uses a read-only refresh token and can only observe and report state, not send commands.

Does it forward Sentry's camera recordings?

No. It alerts on the on/off state only. Recordings stay on the car's USB drive and in the Tesla app.

What other alerts come with it?

Charging, low battery, TPMS/tyre pressure, car left unlocked, and drive-ended summaries with cost — all in the same $5/month plan.

Is my Tesla password shared with WattsonBot?

No. You log in on Tesla's own page; WattsonBot only ever receives a revocable refresh token.

What does it cost?
