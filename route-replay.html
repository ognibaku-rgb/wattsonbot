01 / What you're looking at

Every drive, recorded — nothing to set up.

WattsonBot polls your Tesla a few times a minute while it's moving. That's enough to reconstruct the whole route, colour it by speed, and mark where the car ended up plugged in. The trip above is a synthetic example, but the data shape is exactly what a real drive produces.

02 / Where it lives

In the Telegram chat, right after you park.

You don't open a dashboard for this. When a drive ends, WattsonBot sends a trip summary — distance, duration, efficiency, cost — with a "Trip route" button that returns the speed-coloured map. Battery, TPMS, Sentry Mode and charging alerts land in the same chat.

03 / FAQ

Fair questions.

Is this a real trip?

No — the drive on this page is generated, so it doesn't expose anyone's real address. A genuine trip produces the same fields: GPS track, per-point speed, and state of charge.

Do I get this exact interactive player?

The bot sends a rendered map image to Telegram after each drive. This page is a demo of the underlying data; a full in-chat interactive replay is on the roadmap.

How accurate is the track?

It's polled, not streamed — a point every 20–30 seconds normally, faster while driving. Enough for an accurate route and speed profile, not for centimetre precision.

Does WattsonBot store my location history?

Trip data lives in your own isolated database instance and is used to answer your chat commands. See the Privacy Policy for retention details.
